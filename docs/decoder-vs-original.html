Earlier tests: random frames, on the sets used to tune the decoder

Same comparison on our first test audio: 44 sets of random 12-character frames, not real messages, made deliberately weak at the levels where decoders start failing. The decoder's settings were chosen on many of these sets, and most of their levels sit near the decoding limit, so the gains here are larger than on the real-traffic test above. Three decoders: the original (commit cba17f3), ours in Normal mode and ours with Deep on (commit 4f21eb5). Measured 2026-11-21; times re-measured for all three decoders in one run.

Every decoder was given the same signals in files. The 95% range is how high or low the true rate could be, given how few wrong messages turned up (exact Poisson range).

Original test audio vs new harder audio (drift and clock off)

Tested 2026-11-21, all three decoders in Normal mode. Question: do the gains over the original JS8Core decoder hold when the test audio has two real-world faults, frequency drift and station clocks that are off?

  • Original test audio: the 12 computer-made test sets listed below, the same files the other results on this page use, made by our test generator (scorecard\synth_gen.cpp) on 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20 and unchanged since. Every station sends on a frequency that holds perfectly steady; every station's clock is right (each message starts 0 to 0.8 s into the cycle); the background is plain random noise; the fading sets use the ITU standard fading model, 1 Hz spread, 2 ms delay.
  • New audio: original + drift: a copy of the original test audio, remade on 2026-11-21 from the same starting values, so the same messages, frequencies, start times and noise. The only difference: each station's frequency slides steadily during its message, at a rate picked at random up to 0.25 Hz per second (up or down).
  • New audio: original + clock off: a copy of the original test audio, remade on 2026-11-21 the same way. The only difference: each station's clock is wrong by an amount picked at random up to 1 second (early or late), so its message starts -1.0 to +1.8 s into the cycle.
  • New audio: original + drift + clock off: a copy of the original test audio with both differences at once.
